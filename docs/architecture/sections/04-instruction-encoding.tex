Instruction encoding is provisional in VIV-32 v0.1. The encoding described in this chapter is intended to support the initial assembler, reference emulator, and hardware implementation.

\section{Instruction Word}

\begin{itemize}
\item VIV-32 instructions are fixed-width 32-bit words.
\item Instruction fetch addresses must be 4-byte aligned.
\item Instruction words are stored in memory using the architecture's big-endian byte order.
\item Instruction encoding diagrams number bits from bit 31, the most significant bit, to bit 0, the least significant bit.
\item Unless otherwise stated, immediate fields are sign-extended to 32 bits before use.
\end{itemize}

For example, the instruction word \texttt{0x12345678} stored at address \texttt{A} is represented in memory as follows:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{ll}
\hline
Address & Byte value \\
\hline
\texttt{A + 0} & \texttt{0x12} \\
\texttt{A + 1} & \texttt{0x34} \\
\texttt{A + 2} & \texttt{0x56} \\
\texttt{A + 3} & \texttt{0x78} \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\section{Major Opcode Field}

\begin{itemize}
\item Bits 31--26 contain the major opcode.
\item The major opcode is 6 bits wide.
\item A 6-bit major opcode provides 64 primary opcode classes.
\item Individual opcode classes may define additional function fields.
\item Opcode space is reserved for future extensions.
\end{itemize}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\hline
Bits & Field & Description \\
\hline
31--26 & opcode & Major opcode \\
25--0  & payload & Format-specific instruction payload \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\section{Register Fields}

\begin{itemize}
\item VIV-32 has 16 general-purpose registers.
\item Register fields are 4 bits wide.
\item Register field value \texttt{0x0} selects (r0).
\item Register field value \texttt{0xF} selects (r15).
\end{itemize}

\texttt{0x1} & (r1) \\

\section{Instruction Format Families}

VIV-32 v0.1 defines a small number of instruction format families. The exact opcode assignments are defined separately in the opcode map.

\subsection{R-Type: Register-Register Operations}

R-type instructions operate primarily on registers.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{llllll}
\hline
Bits & 31--26 & 25--22 & 21--18 & 17--14 & 13--0 \\
\hline
Field & opcode & rd & ra & rb & func/reserved \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\begin{itemize}
\item (rd) is the destination register.
\item (ra) is the first source register.
\item (rb) is the second source register.
\item The remaining bits select a sub-operation or are reserved.
\item Arithmetic and logical R-type instructions update condition flags by default.
\end{itemize}

Examples of R-type operations include:

\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{add rd, ra, rb}
\item \texttt{sub rd, ra, rb}
\item \texttt{and rd, ra, rb}
\item \texttt{or rd, ra, rb}
\item \texttt{xor rd, ra, rb}
\end{itemize}

\subsection{R2-Type: Register-Register Operations with Two Destinations}

R2-type instructions are used for operations that naturally produce two results, such as full-width multiplication or division with remainder.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lllllll}
\hline
Bits & 31--26 & 25--22 & 21--18 & 17--14 & 13--10 & 9--0 \\
\hline
Field & opcode & rd0 & rd1 & ra & rb & func/reserved \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\begin{itemize}
\item (rd0) is the first destination register.
\item (rd1) is the second destination register.
\item (ra) is the first source register.
\item (rb) is the second source register.
\item The remaining bits select a sub-operation or are reserved.
\end{itemize}

Examples of R2-type operations include:

\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{mul rdlo, rdhi, ra, rb}
\item \texttt{mulu rdlo, rdhi, ra, rb}
\item \texttt{div rdq, rdr, ra, rb}
\item \texttt{divu rdq, rdr, ra, rb}
\end{itemize}

For multiplication, (rd0) receives the low 32 bits and (rd1) receives the high 32 bits. For division, (rd0) receives the quotient and (rd1) receives the remainder.

\subsection{I-Type: Register-Immediate Operations}

I-type instructions operate on a register and an immediate value.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lllll}
\hline
Bits & 31--26 & 25--22 & 21--18 & 17--0 \\
\hline
Field & opcode & rd & ra & imm18 \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\begin{itemize}
\item (rd) is the destination register.
\item (ra) is the source register.
\item \texttt{imm18} is an 18-bit immediate value.
\item Unless otherwise stated, \texttt{imm18} is sign-extended to 32 bits before use.
\item Arithmetic and logical I-type instructions update condition flags by default.
\end{itemize}

Examples of I-type operations include:

\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{addi rd, ra, imm}
\item \texttt{andi rd, ra, imm}
\item \texttt{ori rd, ra, imm}
\item \texttt{xori rd, ra, imm}
\end{itemize}

\subsection{U-Type: Upper Immediate and Constant Construction}

U-type instructions are used to construct larger constants and addresses in registers.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lllll}
\hline
Bits & 31--26 & 25--22 & 21--16 & 15--0 \\
\hline
Field & opcode & rd & mode/reserved & imm16 \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\begin{itemize}
\item (rd) is the destination register.
\item \texttt{imm16} is a 16-bit immediate value.
\item U-type instructions are intended to support full 32-bit constant and address construction.
\item The exact semantics of U-type instructions are defined in the instruction set chapter.
\end{itemize}

A typical full-width constant may be constructed using an upper-immediate instruction followed by a lower-immediate operation, for example:

\begin{verbatim}
lui  r1, 0x1234
ori  r1, r1, 0x5678
\end{verbatim}

This sequence constructs the value \texttt{0x12345678} in (r1), subject to the exact semantics of \texttt{lui} and \texttt{ori}.

\section{Memory Instruction Formats}

\subsection{M-Type: Load and Store Operations}

M-type instructions access memory using base-plus-offset addressing.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{llllll}
\hline
Bits & 31--26 & 25--22 & 21--18 & 17--16 & 15--0 \\
\hline
Field & opcode & rd/rs & base & size & offset16 \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\begin{itemize}
\item For loads, (rd/rs) is the destination register.
\item For stores, (rd/rs) is the source register.
\item (base) is the base address register.
\item \texttt{offset16} is a signed 16-bit byte offset.
\item The effective address is (base + signextend(offset16)).
\item The \texttt{size} field selects the access size.
\end{itemize}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\hline
Size field & Access size & Description \\
\hline
\texttt{00} & 8-bit  & byte \\
\texttt{01} & 16-bit & halfword \\
\texttt{10} & 32-bit & word \\
\texttt{11} & reserved & reserved \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Examples of M-type operations include:

\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{lb rd, offset(base)}
\item \texttt{lbu rd, offset(base)}
\item \texttt{lh rd, offset(base)}
\item \texttt{lhu rd, offset(base)}
\item \texttt{lw rd, offset(base)}
\item \texttt{sb rs, offset(base)}
\item \texttt{sh rs, offset(base)}
\item \texttt{sw rs, offset(base)}
\end{itemize}

\section{Branch and Jump Formats}

VIV-32 supports both flag-based conditional branches and register-comparison branches.

\subsection{BF-Type: Flag-Based Conditional Branches}

BF-type instructions branch according to the current condition flags in the status register.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{llll}
\hline
Bits & 31--26 & 25--22 & 21--0 \\
\hline
Field & opcode & cond & offset22 \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{cond} selects a condition-code test.
\item \texttt{offset22} is a signed PC-relative branch offset.
\item Branch offsets are measured in 32-bit instruction words.
\item The encoded offset is shifted left by two bits to form a byte offset.
\item The branch target is relative to the address of the instruction following the branch.
\end{itemize}

Examples of flag-based branches include:

\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{b.eq label}
\item \texttt{b.ne label}
\item \texttt{b.cs label}
\item \texttt{b.cc label}
\item \texttt{b.vs label}
\item \texttt{b.vc label}
\item \texttt{b.es label}
\item \texttt{b.ec label}
\end{itemize}

\subsection{BC-Type: Register-Comparison Branches}

BC-type instructions compare two registers and branch according to the specified comparison.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{llllll}
\hline
Bits & 31--26 & 25--22 & 21--18 & 17--14 & 13--0 \\
\hline
Field & opcode & cond & ra & rb & offset14 \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{cond} selects the comparison condition.
\item (ra) is the first comparison register.
\item (rb) is the second comparison register.
\item \texttt{offset14} is a signed PC-relative branch offset.
\item Branch offsets are measured in 32-bit instruction words.
\item The encoded offset is shifted left by two bits to form a byte offset.
\item The branch target is relative to the address of the instruction following the branch.
\item Register-comparison branches may update condition flags if defined by the instruction set chapter.
\end{itemize}

Examples of register-comparison branches include:

\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{beq ra, rb, label}
\item \texttt{bne ra, rb, label}
\item \texttt{blt ra, rb, label}
\item \texttt{bge ra, rb, label}
\item \texttt{bltu ra, rb, label}
\item \texttt{bgeu ra, rb, label}
\end{itemize}

\subsection{J-Type: PC-Relative Jump and Call}

J-type instructions perform unconditional PC-relative control transfer.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\hline
Bits & 31--26 & 25--0 \\
\hline
Field & opcode & offset26 \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{offset26} is a signed PC-relative offset.
\item Jump offsets are measured in 32-bit instruction words.
\item The encoded offset is shifted left by two bits to form a byte offset.
\item The jump target is relative to the address of the instruction following the jump.
\end{itemize}

Examples of J-type operations include:

\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{jmp label}
\item \texttt{call label}
\end{itemize}

\subsection{JR-Type: Register-Indirect Jump and Call}

JR-type instructions perform register-indirect control transfer.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lllll}
\hline
Bits & 31--26 & 25--22 & 21--18 & 17--0 \\
\hline
Field & opcode & rd/link & target & func/reserved \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\begin{itemize}
\item (target) contains the target address.
\item For plain register jumps, (rd/link) is ignored or must be (r0).
\item For register-indirect calls, (rd/link) receives the return address.
\item The standard ABI link register is (r15).
\item Register-indirect targets must be 4-byte aligned.
\end{itemize}

Examples of JR-type operations include:

\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{jr target}
\item \texttt{jalr rd, target}
\item \texttt{ret}
\end{itemize}

The \texttt{ret} instruction may be encoded as a distinct instruction or defined as an assembler pseudo-instruction for \texttt{jr r15}.

\section{System Instruction Format}

\subsection{X-Type: System and Control Operations}

X-type instructions access system facilities, control registers, traps, and processor state.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lllll}
\hline
Bits & 31--26 & 25--22 & 21--18 & 17--0 \\
\hline
Field & opcode & rd & rs & sysfunc \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\begin{itemize}
\item (rd) is a destination register when required.
\item (rs) is a source register when required.
\item \texttt{sysfunc} selects the system operation.
\end{itemize}

Examples of X-type operations include:

\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{nop}
\item \texttt{halt}
\item \texttt{trap imm}
\item \texttt{syscall}
\item \texttt{iret}
\item \texttt{ei}
\item \texttt{di}
\item \texttt{rdpc rd}
\item \texttt{mrs rd, sr}
\item \texttt{msr sr, rs}
\end{itemize}

The \texttt{syscall} instruction is a specialised software trap used by the operating environment. It may share trap machinery internally, but it is exposed as a distinct assembly instruction.

\section{NOP Encoding}

\begin{itemize}
\item The all-zero instruction word \texttt{0x00000000} encodes \texttt{nop}.
\item \texttt{nop} performs no architectural state change except advancing the program counter.
\item \texttt{nop} does not modify condition flags.
\end{itemize}

\section{Flag Update Policy}

\begin{itemize}
\item Arithmetic and logical instructions update condition flags by default.
\item Comparison instructions update condition flags without storing a result.
\item Load and store instructions do not update condition flags.
\item Branch, jump, call, and return instructions do not update condition flags.
\item System instructions update condition flags only when explicitly specified.
\end{itemize}

\section{Reserved Encodings}

\begin{itemize}
\item Encodings not defined by VIV-32 v0.1 are reserved.
\item Reserved encodings must not be generated by conforming assemblers.
\item Executing a reserved encoding has illegal-instruction behaviour.
\item In VIV-32 v0.1, illegal instructions halt the machine.
\end{itemize}
